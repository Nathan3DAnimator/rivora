--- pt
Um nível feito **durante o jogo** (salas procedurais, um gráfico PCG, atores Static criados por Spawn Actor) recebe a mesma iluminação do **Build Lighting** do editor, e tudo o que o Details edita também muda em jogo.
1. **Build Lighting (Async)**: lightmaps e o volume de sondas de toda malha **Static**. **Mode = Loading Screen**: o jogo espera atrás da tela de carregamento do projeto e o bake usa a GPU inteira (~30 ms por quadro). **Mode = Progressive**: o jogo continua; uma fatia do bake por quadro dentro de **Budget ms** (1 ms por padrão, medido pelos próprios timestamps), sem travadas: a preparação roda numa thread e o envio à GPU vai em fatias. Até terminar, a **GI dinâmica** ilumina o nível; os lightmaps aparecem no fim. A primeira saída segue na hora; **Completed** quando acaba. **Event On Lighting Built** (Success, Seconds) roda em todo Blueprint; **Get Lighting Build Progress** dá a barra; **Cancel Lighting Build** para.
2. **Save Built Lighting / Load Built Lighting**: guarda o resultado com um nome (por exemplo a semente do mapa): o mesmo mapa gerado de novo carrega a luz sem refazer o bake.
3. **Set / Get Property by Name**: qualquer campo do Details pelo rótulo, no ator ou nos componentes dele: "Intensity", "Attenuation Radius", "Max Walk Speed", "Light.Color" (Seção.Rótulo quando dois componentes têm o mesmo). **List Properties** lista os que o ator tem.
4. **Set / Get Console Variable** e **Execute Console Command** ("r.ScreenPercentage 75"): render e qualidade (r.GI.Probes, r.Reflections, r.Shadow.Virtual.Resolution, r.RenderStyle, sg.Scalability), física (p.GravityZ, p.SolverSubsteps) e áudio (au.MasterVolume, au.Binaural). No Play do editor, os ajustes voltam ao parar. No jogo empacotado: `--cvar r.GI.Probes=0` na linha de comando.
5. O que ainda é **só do editor** sai da varredura `tools/ingame_sweep.py` (a lista fica em notas-dev/em-jogo.md).

--- en
A level made **while playing** (procedural rooms, a PCG graph, Static actors made by Spawn Actor) gets the same lighting as the editor's **Build Lighting**, and everything the Details panel edits changes in the game too.
1. **Build Lighting (Async)**: the lightmaps and the probe volume of every **Static** mesh. **Mode = Loading Screen**: the game waits behind the project's loading screen and the bake takes the whole GPU (~30 ms a frame). **Mode = Progressive**: the game plays on; a slice of the bake each frame inside **Budget ms** (1 ms by default, measured by its own timestamps), without hitches: the preparation runs on a thread and the upload to the GPU goes in slices. Until it ends the **dynamic GI** lights the level; the lightmaps show at the end. The first output goes on at once; **Completed** when it ends. **Event On Lighting Built** (Success, Seconds) runs in every Blueprint; **Get Lighting Build Progress** gives the bar; **Cancel Lighting Build** stops it.
2. **Save Built Lighting / Load Built Lighting**: keeps the result under a name (the map's seed, say): the same map made again loads its light without baking.
3. **Set / Get Property by Name**: any Details field by its label, on the actor or its components: "Intensity", "Attenuation Radius", "Max Walk Speed", "Light.Color" (Section.Label when two components share one). **List Properties** lists the ones the actor has.
4. **Set / Get Console Variable** and **Execute Console Command** ("r.ScreenPercentage 75"): render and quality (r.GI.Probes, r.Reflections, r.Shadow.Virtual.Resolution, r.RenderStyle, sg.Scalability), physics (p.GravityZ, p.SolverSubsteps) and audio (au.MasterVolume, au.Binaural). In the editor's Play the settings come back when it stops. In the packaged game: `--cvar r.GI.Probes=0` on the command line.
5. What is still **editor only** comes out of the sweep `tools/ingame_sweep.py` (its list is in notas-dev/em-jogo.md).
